UNDERSTANDING THE RESEARCH NEED

From an initial question to a clear evidence direction

01. From an Unanswered Clinical Question to a Research Program
Research Strategy

THE CHALLENGE

A clinical, scientific, or product-related question has been identified, but it is not yet clear what research is needed to answer it. The challenge is to move from a broad area of interest to a focused question that can be addressed through a structured research program.

OUR APPROACH

We begin by understanding the question, reviewing the available evidence, and identifying what remains unknown. The evidence gap is then translated into a focused research objective and an appropriate pathway to generate the required evidence.

THE OUTCOME

A clearly defined research direction built around a meaningful and answerable question. The client gains a structured foundation for deciding which research to undertake next.

02. From Emerging Product Questions to a Clear Evidence Strategy
Evidence Strategy

THE CHALLENGE

As a product enters clinical practice or becomes established, multiple evidence questions may emerge regarding its use, outcomes, patient populations, or its place in practice. The challenge is not simply conducting another study, but deciding which questions to prioritize and where further evidence would add the greatest value.

OUR APPROACH

We help map the existing evidence against emerging questions and identify the most relevant evidence gaps. Priority questions are then translated into a focused evidence-generation strategy, considering what research may be needed over the product lifecycle.

THE OUTCOME

A clearer and more focused evidence strategy rather than a collection of isolated research ideas. Research priorities are aligned around the questions where additional evidence can make the greatest scientific or clinical contribution.

03. When Research Needs to Be Designed for Publication from the Start
Publication-Focused Evidence Generation

THE CHALLENGE

Research may be intended to contribute to the scientific literature and inform broader scientific discussion. If this intended use is considered only after the study is complete, important decisions about the question, design, outcomes, and data may already limit the scientific value of the research.

OUR APPROACH

We consider the intended scientific contribution while shaping the research question, methodology, outcomes, and evidence-generation plan. The study is designed to support meaningful scientific communication based on the evidence generated.

THE OUTCOME

A research program with a clearer link between the evidence it is designed to generate and its intended scientific contribution. Publication is considered an outcome of well-planned research rather than an afterthought once the study is complete.

04. Generating the Right Evidence Without Overdesigning the Research
Fit-for-Purpose Research

THE CHALLENGE

An important evidence need does not always require the largest, longest, or most complex possible study. The challenge is to generate evidence that is sufficient and meaningful for the question being asked without introducing unnecessary complexity, scale, cost, or burden.

OUR APPROACH

We focus on the specific question the research needs to answer and identify the essential design elements required to address it. Study scope, population, outcomes, follow-up, and operational requirements are then aligned to create a proportionate and efficient research model.

THE OUTCOME

A fit-for-purpose research approach focused on generating the evidence that matters without unnecessary complexity. Resources and effort are directed toward the elements most important to the research objective.

DESIGNING THE RIGHT RESEARCH PROGRAM

Choosing and shaping the right approach to generate evidence

05. Making a Strong Research Idea Feasible to Execute
Study Feasibility & Design

THE CHALLENGE

A research concept may be scientifically compelling but difficult to implement as originally envisioned. Recruitment challenges, limited patient access, demanding follow-up requirements, complex assessments, or site-level burden may put successful study conduct at risk.

OUR APPROACH

We assess whether the proposed research can realistically be delivered across the intended patient population and clinical settings. Feasibility considerations are used to refine the study design, site model, assessments, and operational requirements while preserving the core scientific objective.

THE OUTCOME

A research design that is both scientifically purposeful and realistically executable. Potential barriers are identified and addressed during planning rather than becoming major challenges after the study begins.

06. Moving From Study Decision to Rapid Study Set-Up
Rapid Study Set-Up

THE CHALLENGE

Once the decision to conduct a study has been made, delays in moving from planning to implementation can affect timelines and momentum. The challenge is to establish the scientific, operational, site, and data foundations of the study efficiently while ensuring that critical preparation is not compromised.

OUR APPROACH

We develop a coordinated study start-up pathway that brings together the key activities required before study conduct begins. Study documentation, operational planning, site readiness, investigator coordination, training, and data-capture preparation are managed through a structured, time-conscious framework.

THE OUTCOME

A more efficient pathway from study decision to study readiness. Key start-up activities are coordinated to help the research program move toward implementation without losing alignment with its scientific and operational requirements.

07. Bringing Fragmented Research Activities Into One Coordinated Program
Integrated Research Support

THE CHALLENGE

A research program may involve different partners or teams for scientific planning, study operations, data management, statistics, and other activities. Multiple handoffs and disconnected responsibilities can create inefficiencies and make it difficult to maintain alignment across the research program.

OUR APPROACH

We bring the required research capabilities together into a single coordinated program structure, with clear connections among planning, execution, data, and analysis. Activities are managed around shared objectives and defined interfaces rather than as isolated deliverables.

THE OUTCOME

A more integrated and coordinated way of managing a research program. Reduced fragmentation and clearer continuity across activities help maintain alignment from planning through study completion.

08. Understanding What Happens in Routine Clinical Practice
Real-World Evidence

THE CHALLENGE

Important questions may exist about how a disease is managed, how care is delivered, how treatments are used, or what outcomes are observed in everyday clinical practice. The objective is to understand routine clinical reality rather than evaluate a single product at a specific stage of its lifecycle.

OUR APPROACH

We define the specific real-world question and develop an appropriate observational research approach around the relevant population, clinical setting, variables, and outcomes. The study is designed to capture evidence from routine practice while systematically maintaining methodological rigor.

THE OUTCOME

A structured pathway for generating evidence about real-world clinical practice. The research can provide insights into patterns, experiences, and outcomes that may not be fully visible through controlled research settings.

09. Following Patients Over Time to Understand Outcomes
Prospective Longitudinal Research

THE CHALLENGE

A single assessment may not adequately answer questions about how patients, treatment patterns, or clinical outcomes change over time. The research requires structured observation across a defined period to understand the course, progression, or evolution of the outcomes of interest.

OUR APPROACH

We develop a prospective research model defining the study population, observation period, follow-up schedule, key variables, and outcomes. Study workflows and data collection are structured to support consistent follow-up throughout the planned observation period.

THE OUTCOME

A longitudinal evidence-generation program capable of examining changes and outcomes over time. The resulting evidence provides a more complete view of the research question than a single point-in-time assessment.

10. Turning Existing Clinical Experience Into Research Evidence
Retrospective Research & Medical Record Review

THE CHALLENGE

Years of routine clinical practice may have generated valuable patient records and clinical experience, but this information has never been systematically examined to answer a defined research question. The challenge is to determine whether available historical information can support the generation of meaningful, reliable evidence.

OUR APPROACH

We define the research question and establish an appropriate retrospective framework, including the study population, eligibility criteria, variables, data sources, standardized data-extraction methods, data-quality review, and analytical plan. The approach is shaped around what evidence can realistically and reliably be generated from the available records.

THE OUTCOME

A structured pathway for transforming existing clinical information into interpretable research evidence. Historical records are organized around a defined scientific question rather than remaining untapped sources of insight.

11. Designing Data Capture Around the Research Question
Clinical Data Management

THE CHALLENGE

A prospective study may require extensive information, but collecting every possible variable can increase investigator burden and affect data quality. At the same time, the absence of critical information can limit the study's ability to answer its intended research question.

OUR APPROACH

We translate the protocol objectives and planned analyses into a focused CRF/eCRF and data-capture framework. Required variables, visit schedules, validation checks, and data-quality controls are aligned to the evidence the study is intended to generate.

THE OUTCOME

A focused data-capture framework that supports consistent collection of the information needed to answer the research question. The study is designed to generate reliable, analysis-ready data without unnecessary data collection.

GENERATING EVIDENCE IN SPECIFIC CLINICAL CONTEXTS

Addressing evidence needs shaped by geography, product lifecycle, or research ownership

12. When Global Evidence Does Not Fully Answer the Indian Question
Indian Evidence Generation

THE CHALLENGE

A therapy or product may already have an established international evidence base, while questions remain about its use, patient population, treatment patterns, or outcomes in Indian clinical practice. Global evidence may not fully reflect the diversity of patients, practice patterns, or healthcare settings in India.

OUR APPROACH

We define the specific local evidence gap and develop a research approach around the Indian clinical context. The study population, sites, variables, and outcomes are designed to generate evidence relevant to the research question in routine healthcare settings in India.

THE OUTCOME

A structured research pathway for generating evidence relevant to Indian clinical practice. The resulting research helps address questions that cannot be fully answered by extrapolating from evidence generated elsewhere.

13. When New Evidence Is Needed After a Product Enters Routine Practice
Post-Marketing Research

THE CHALLENGE

A product may be in routine clinical use, but a specific question has emerged that requires further structured investigation. Unlike broader evidence planning, the need is now to design and conduct research around a clearly identified post-marketing question.

OUR APPROACH

We develop an appropriate research program around the specific question, defining the study population, design, outcomes, and implementation model. The scientific and operational approach is aligned to generate evidence relevant to the product's use in routine clinical practice.

THE OUTCOME

A focused post-marketing research program designed to answer a defined question arising after product introduction. The study generates new evidence around a specific area of uncertainty rather than broadly exploring multiple evidence needs.

14. A Strong Research Idea Without the Infrastructure to Execute It
Investigator-Initiated Research

THE CHALLENGE

An investigator may have an important clinical question and a strong research idea but lack the infrastructure, systems, or coordinated support needed to turn it into a formal research program. The challenge is not in identifying the question, but in enabling the investigator to conduct the research effectively.

OUR APPROACH

We provide the research support needed to translate the concept into an executable study, bringing together appropriate scientific, operational, data, and analytical capabilities. The support model is structured around the requirements of the specific investigator-led research program.

THE OUTCOME

A clearer and more executable pathway from investigator idea to completed research. The investigator can access coordinated support to generate evidence without having to build the entire research infrastructure independently.

TAKING RESEARCH ACROSS MULTIPLE SETTINGS

Expanding the scientific reach of research and coordinating its execution

15. When the Research Question Requires Evidence From Diverse Healthcare Settings
Multicenter Evidence Generation

THE CHALLENGE

A research question may require evidence from a broader range of patient populations, clinical environments, or healthcare settings. Evidence generated within a limited set of similar sites may not provide the breadth needed to understand the question across varied real-world contexts.

OUR APPROACH

We develop a research model that intentionally incorporates appropriate diversity across participating settings while maintaining a common study framework. Site profile, patient population, geography, and clinical environment are considered in relation to the scientific objective.

THE OUTCOME

A broader evidence-generation framework designed to examine the research question across varied healthcare settings. The research captures a wider range of clinical experience while maintaining a consistent methodological approach.

16. Bringing Research Together Across Multiple Centers
Multicenter Research

THE CHALLENGE

A multicenter study has been identified as the appropriate approach, but participating centers need to operate within a common research framework. Differences in site processes, investigator experience, and clinical environments can make consistent implementation challenging.

OUR APPROACH

We support the planning and coordinated setup of the multicenter study, aligning participating centers around common study procedures, documentation, training, responsibilities, and data workflows. The focus is on creating the operational foundation needed for consistent study conduct across sites.

THE OUTCOME

A coordinated multicenter research program with participating centers working within a common scientific and operational framework. The study is prepared for consistent implementation across the site network.

17. Launching a Large-Scale Study Across 100+ Sites
Large-Scale Clinical Research

THE CHALLENGE

A research program requires participation from more than 100 investigators or clinical sites to achieve the required geographic reach, patient access, or breadth of evidence. At this scale, the challenge extends beyond study design to establishing a structured, coordinated system to activate a large network.

OUR APPROACH

We develop a scalable implementation model that covers site identification and feasibility assessment, investigator onboarding, documentation, training, site activation, and centralized study coordination. Standardized processes and clearly defined workflows are designed to support efficient rollout while maintaining consistency across a large and geographically distributed site network.

THE OUTCOME

A structured large-scale research program with a coordinated framework for activating and managing 100+ participating sites. The study is positioned to generate broad evidence while maintaining operational visibility and standardized execution.

18. Keeping a Multicenter Study Coordinated From First Patient to Completion
Multicenter Study Management

THE CHALLENGE

Once a multicenter study is underway, participating sites may progress at different rates and encounter different operational challenges. Recruitment, follow-up, documentation, data queries, timelines, and site-level issues require ongoing coordination throughout the study.

OUR APPROACH

We establish structured workflows for site communication, study tracking, recruitment and follow-up oversight, remote monitoring where applicable, query management, and operational issue resolution. Progress across the study is reviewed to identify emerging issues and support timely action.

THE OUTCOME

A more controlled and coordinated study environment throughout active conduct. Sponsors and study teams gain clearer visibility of progress, challenges, and actions needed to keep the research program moving toward completion.
